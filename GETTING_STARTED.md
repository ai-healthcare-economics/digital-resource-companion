# Getting Started

**Release V1.0**

## Use the simplest entry point

- New to the companion: open `START_HERE.html` or `START_HERE.pdf`.
- Working on a real institutional task: use `GUIDED_PATHWAYS.md`.
- Need several forms offline: use `bundles/`.
- Learning a method: begin with a worked example.
- Conducting quantitative analysis: use `PYTHON_ANALYTICS.md`.
- Maintaining or integrating the source: use the raw Chapter folders, catalogs, schemas, process files, Python, and LaTeX.

## Minimum sequence for operational use

1. Define the decision, population, setting, accountable authority, intended and excluded uses, comparator, time horizon, and consequences of error.
2. Read the aligned book chapter and select the smallest sufficient resource set.
3. Create a governed local copy; do not edit the public master directly.
4. Replace synthetic or generic material with authorized, versioned local evidence.
5. Separate observations, modelled estimates, value judgements, and contractual claims.
6. Assign accountable owners and identify mandatory safeguards that cannot be offset by favourable scores elsewhere.
7. Record uncertainty, subgroup effects, workload, affordability, continuity, and exit proportionate to consequence.
8. End with a dated decision, conditions, monitoring thresholds, review triggers, and stopping authority.

## File formats

- **DOCX/ODT** — narrative records, committee papers, and editable forms.
- **XLSX/ODS** — registers, repeated records, calculations, and scenario analysis.
- **CSV/JSON** — structured exchange and reproducible workflows.
- **Browser forms** — orientation and structured drafting; transfer approved work to a governed institutional record.
- **Python/Jupyter** — advanced quantitative analysis.
- **LaTeX** — publication source and advanced teaching material.

## Windows path length

Clone or extract the repository to a short local path, such as `C:\AIHE\companion`, because deeply nested canonical source folders may exceed limits supported by some applications.

## Before use

Confirm the applicable clinical, legal, regulatory, privacy, cybersecurity, procurement, accreditation, quality, and health technology assessment requirements. These resources support structured analysis and documentation; they do not replace professional review or institutional authorization.
