Interpretation aid

Scenario

A fictional health system evaluates an enterprise documentation and workflow platform over five years. The proposal combines a base licence with volume-linked fees and requires substantial integration, training, monitoring, and update validation. Benefits ramp gradually and depend on adoption and whether released time is actually used.

What this example demonstrates

  • Build a complete lifecycle cost profile.
  • Distinguish fixed, variable, internal, and exit costs.
  • Model benefit ramp-up and pricing exposure.
  • Calculate ROI, break-even, and downside conditions.

Worked interpretation

Year one is strongly negative because integration and data work precede benefit realization. Later years improve, but the five-year result remains sensitive to volume-linked fees and the share of time converted into usable capacity. The organization should cap variable fees, price integration and monitoring explicitly, and link renewal to verified local benefits through a staged contract with a break clause.
